# Project 4 · Precedent Transactions — IT Services (2014–2024)

**Files:** `IT_Services_Precedents.xlsx` · `IT_Services_Precedents.pdf` · `build_model.py`

## What it is

A fourteen-deal precedent set for the IT-services sector, built from announcement press releases and filings rather than exported from a database: Capgemini–IGATE (2015), Atos–Syntel (2018), Capgemini–Altran (2019), Wipro–Capco (2021), Carlyle–Hexaware (2021), LTI–Mindtree (2022), Infosys–in-tech (2024), Cognizant–Belcan (2024) and six others. For each: enterprise value, target LTM revenue and EBITDA, implied multiples, one-day premium for public targets, consideration, source and a note on data quality. Statistics overall and by acquirer type and target geography; the control premium relative to Project 3's trading multiples; an illustrative implied range for TCS.

## Reading it honestly

* **Part of that "control premium" is the cycle, not control.** The precedents were struck in 2014–2022 when the sector traded at 20–30× earnings; Project 3's trading multiples are from September 2026 after a de-rating to 10–15×. Comparing deal multiples from one period with trading multiples from another overstates the premium. The clean version of this analysis pairs each deal with the trading multiples *at its announcement date*; that is listed under upgrades.
* **The one-day premia look low** (median 4.6%) because two targets (IGATE, Syntel) had already run up on takeover speculation, and one transaction (LTI–Mindtree) was a merger under common control with an exchange ratio set at market. Measured against a one-month or three-month unaffected price the premia are 20–25%. The table says which is which.
* **Dispersion is the message.** Mid-cap services assets cluster at 1.5–3.5× revenue; TriZetto (3.8×) and Mindtree (4.8×) had platform or scarcity value. A seller should see the whole distribution before hearing a number.
* **Data quality is stated, not hidden.** Several private-target revenue figures come from rounded press-release disclosures and are marked approximate. Medians and quartiles are used because they tolerate a single imprecise entry; the count of observations is shown for every statistic. Anyone using a figure from this table in a client document should verify it against the cited source.
* **The TCS range is illustrative.** No target here is within an order of magnitude of TCS's size; the range exists to complete the football field and to quantify the sector's control premium, not to price a takeover of TCS.

## What I would do with more time

* Pair every deal with same-date trading multiples to separate control premium from cycle.
* Add the proxy-statement disclosures for the US public deals (IGATE, Syntel): the target's own bankers' fairness analyses give exact LTM metrics and the premium bases.
* Extend the set with 2025–2026 deals as they are disclosed, and with engineering-R&D services (a distinct sub-sector with higher multiples).
